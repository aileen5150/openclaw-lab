## The Actual Recommendations

### Best for Home Backup / Apartment Living: Jackery Explorer 1000 v2

At 23.8 lbs, this is the only unit in its class you can realistically grab and go in an emergency. Third-party testing from *The Wirecutter* and *RTINGS.com* consistently shows Jackery's pure sine wave inverter handles sensitive electronics (CPAP machines, laptop chargers, networking equipment) without degradation. The 1,002 Wh capacity will run a full refrigerator for 8–12 hours — real-world data from Jackery's own field tests shows approximately 1.5 kWh daily draw for a standard French-door fridge. That's almost a full day of food protection. At $849, it's the best cost-to-weight ratio in the category. The solar recharging upgrade path ($299 for 100W panel) adds genuine independence.

### Best for Remote Work / Job Site: EcoFlow Delta 3 Plus

The 1,800 W output handles a 15-inch MacBook Pro (96W charging), 27" monitor, router, and phone chargers simultaneously without triggering the overload protection. EcoFlow's X-Stream charging tech — verified by *Tom's Hardware* testing — reaches 80% charge in 50 minutes from a wall outlet. That matters when you're moving between job sites and need a fast turnaround. The expandable ecosystem means you can add a 512 Wh battery pack for surge capacity. Price is competitive with Jackery at $999, and the build quality on the 2024 Delta series addressed the earlier durability complaints.

### Best for Extended Off-Grid / Whole-Home Backup: EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3

This is overkill for most people, but if you live in an area with frequent multi-day outages or work remotely in vehicles, the numbers are compelling. At $3,299, it costs more upfront but the 4,096 Wh capacity at $0.81/Wh is the lowest cost-per-watt-hour in this entire comparison. *Home Battery Expert* testing confirms it can power a window AC unit (800–1,200 W) for 4–6 hours. The 3,600 W output and smart home integration via EcoFlow's app make it a legitimate short-term whole-home bridge. For those on solar, EcoFlow's smart generator mode auto-engages a gas generator when the battery dips below a set threshold — a real automation win.

### Worst Value in the Current Market: Goal Zero Yeti 1000 Core

Goal Zero's 2024 pricing reset didn't go far enough. At $1,199 for 983 Wh, you're paying $1.22 per watt-hour versus Jackery's $0.85. The 1,000 W output is also a full tier below competitors at a comparable price. Their ecosystem is excellent — the Yeti App and integration options are genuinely polished — but the hardware economics don't justify the premium. Skip this unless you already own Goal Zero solar panels and want to maintain compatibility.

## Key Specs That Actually Matter (And Ones That Don't)

**Weight** is more important than the specs sheet suggests. The Jackery 1000 v2 at 23.8 lbs fits in a carry-on luggage compartment and can be carried one-handed. The Anker 767 at 67 lbs requires two people and a cart. If portability is a factor, weight will constrain real-world usability more than any spec.

**Cycle life** is where brands hide real differences. EcoFlow rates the Delta 3 Plus at 3,000+ cycles to 80% capacity. Jackery's newer models claim 4,000 cycles at 80% for the same chemistry (LiFePO4). LiFePO4 chemistry is now standard across all brands in this price tier — avoid any unit still using NMC chemistry, which degrades faster and runs hotter.

**Inverter type** — all recommendations above use pure sine wave inverters. Modified sine wave units (cheaper alternatives) can cause issues with laser printers, some power tool chargers, and audio equipment. Every unit above passes this test.

**Solar input rate** matters more than battery size. The Jackery 1000 v2 accepts up to 200W solar input — meaning a full recharge in 6–8 hours of good sun. The Goal Zero at 120W max solar input is slower. If solar integration is part of your plan, check peak input wattage alongside capacity.
