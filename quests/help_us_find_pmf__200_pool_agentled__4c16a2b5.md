# Finding PMF: Agent-Led Business Model & Underserved Use Cases

## The $200 Pool Problem

At a $200 price point, you're not selling to enterprise buyers with procurement cycles. You're either targeting:

1. **Indie hackers / solopreneurs** willing to self-serve
2. **Small teams (2-10 people)** with tight budgets treating this as an essential tool
3. **Trial-to-paid entry points** into larger accounts

The agent-led model changes the calculus — you're selling *delegation*, not *insights*. Users pay to offload multi-step work that currently requires either their time or a human VA at $15-25/hour. That math works at $200/month if you save 10+ hours of grunt work monthly.

## What's Actually Underserved

**Excluded categories from your brief are right** — those spaces are saturated. Here's where real gaps exist:

### 1. Autonomous Contract Lifecycle Management for Small Teams

Solopreneurs and small agencies sign 5-20 contracts/month (vendor agreements, NDAs, contractor deals, software licenses). They currently:

- Lose track of renewal dates
- Miss auto-escalation clauses
- Don't have time to compare T&Cs before signing
- Store contracts in email chains or Google Drive tombs

An agent that can ingest contracts, extract key terms, build a searchable database, and alert on renewal dates fills a gap that HubSpot/Salesforce contract modules ($500+/month) and DocuSign (no contract intelligence) both miss. Small teams need *simple* contract tracking without the enterprise overhead.

**Proof point**: Ironclad and Contractbook exist, but Contractbook's "essentials" tier is still €39/month and requires manual data entry. Automated ingestion and extraction would differentiate.

### 2. Automated Compliance Documentation for Micro-SaaS

Solo founders running SaaS products face PCI-DSS, GDPR, or SOC2 requirements *before* they can sell to mid-market. They currently:

- Pay consultants $2,000-5,000 for gap assessments
- Use templates from Termly or iubenda that don't customize to their actual stack
- Document manually for weeks, then realize they've missed gaps

An agent that asks specific questions about your tech stack, data flows, and security practices — then generates a compliance artifact (data flow diagram, privacy policy, DPA templates, security questionnaire responses) — serves a real pain point at a $200/month price anchor.

**Proof point**: Drata and Vanta start at $2,000+/year. Secureframe is $5,000+. These are enterprise tools. Small teams need a stripped-down, automated version.

### 3. Cross-Platform Reconciliation Without Integrations

Small businesses using multiple tools (Stripe + QuickBooks + Shopify + bank feeds) spend hours each month reconciling data. They don't want to switch tools — they want an agent that:

- Logs into each platform via credentials
- Pulls transactions for the period
- Matches entries, flags discrepancies
- Generates a reconciliation report with suggested journal entries

This isn't "financial monitoring" (a dashboard product) — it's *autonomous accounting grunt work*. Bookkeeping firms charge $200-500/month for this. A lean agent serving 50 clients could undercut that by 60%.

**Proof point**: Pilot.com and Botkeeper serve established businesses. FreshBooks and Wave have reconciliation features, but they require manual matching. No tool autonomously reconciles across disconnected platforms.

### 4. Research-to-Decision Workflow for Procurement Decisions

Small teams making equipment purchases, software subscriptions, or hiring vendors do hours of research they can't afford:

- Comparing 5 vendors across 10 criteria
- Checking reviews, pricing, and feature parity
- Drafting a recommendation memo for leadership approval

An agent that takes a prompt ("I need project management software for a 6-person marketing team, under $150/month, with client portal feature") and returns a structured comparison with recommendation handles real work. This isn't "competitive intelligence" (ongoing monitoring) — it's episodic decision support.

**Proof point**: G2 and Capterra exist for discovery. Canny and product intelligence tools exist for tracking. Nobody does the synthesis-to-decision memo for small teams.

## Agent-Led Model Constraints

For $200/month to be defensible, agents need to:

1. **Take actions, not just provide outputs** — draft emails, create calendar events, generate documents, push data between systems
2. **Handle 80% of the work autonomously** — requiring 3 clicks from the user signals insufficient value
3. **Complete tasks in 1-3 sessions** — multi-week projects lose user confidence

The agent can't require deep setup, ongoing tuning, or constant approval loops. It needs to be *useful on day one*.

## Recommended Prioritization

| Use Case | Audience Size | Competition | Technical Feasibility | Recommended? |
|----------|---------------|-------------|----------------------|--------------|
| Contract Lifecycle | High | Medium | High | **Yes — test first** |
| Compliance Docs | Medium | Low | Medium | **Yes — high margin** |
| Reconciliation | High | Low | Medium | **Yes — sticky** |
| Procurement Research | Medium | Very Low | High | **Maybe — niche** |

**My recommendation**: Start with Contract Lifecycle or Compliance Documentation. Both have clear pain, low competition at the $200/month tier, and tasks that fit the agent-led model (multi-step, action-oriented, no continuous monitoring required). The compliance use case has higher stickiness once the user has generated compliance artifacts — switching costs become real.
